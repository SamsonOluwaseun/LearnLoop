# Curriculum map

LearnLoop's questions follow what children meet at school in England. Each skill below is linked to a statement in the official curriculum. The questions are original. LearnLoop does not copy any scheme, app or textbook.

## How the starting year is chosen

In England the school year runs from 1 September to 31 August, and a child starts Reception in the September after their fourth birthday. LearnLoop uses the date of birth to work out the school year, then starts each subject there. A child not yet at school starts at Reception. Maths and English go from Reception to Year 6. Reasoning (11+ practice) goes from Year 4 to Year 6 and starts no lower than Year 4.

After the start, only results move things. A birthday or a new school year never makes the questions harder on its own.

## How questions get harder

Every skill has three levels. Level 1 is the gentlest version of the curriculum statement and level 3 is the fullest.

- **A skill moves up a level** when at least 5 of the child's last 6 answers on it were given without any help, across at least 2 different sessions.
- **A skill moves back a level** when 2 or fewer of the last 6 answers were given without help, across at least 2 sessions.
- **The year moves up** for a subject when three quarters of that year's skills have reached level 3.
- **The year moves back** when half of that year's skills are at level 1 and still a struggle across several sessions.
- Answers given after a clue, another way of looking at it, or a worked example never count towards moving up.
- Each session starts with skills to practise again, then skills not seen for a week, then the rest.
- When a question is too hard three times, the child moves on and the skill comes back later with an easier example.

Every change is written to the parent report with its reason, for example: *Taking away within 20 moved up to level 2: 5 of the last 6 answers were given without help, over 2 sessions.* Parents can set the level for each subject by hand in **Children and rules**.

These rules are starting points for a family pilot, not proven teaching rules.

## Reasoning (11+ practice)

Reasoning is a general practice track for families preparing for selective secondary school tests. It is not tied to any area, school or exam board, and parents switch it on per child. It covers the two pillars that most tests add to English and Maths:

- **Verbal reasoning:** letter and number sequences, letter codes, word analogies, odd one out, closest and opposite meanings, compound words, hidden words, balancing sums and letters standing for numbers.
- **Non-verbal reasoning:** shape sequences, odd shape out, mirror images, turning shapes, shape grids and shape analogies. Every shape is drawn by code, and the tests prove that exactly one option fits the rule.

All reasoning questions are original. Published 11+ papers from test providers, CGP, Twinkl and others are copyrighted, so LearnLoop uses none of their questions. Word puzzles are written and reviewed by hand. Hidden words, letter codes, sums and shape puzzles are checked by code.

## Your own spelling list

Parents can add up to 30 words of their own, for example the school's weekly spellings (English programme of study, spelling appendix 1). They come up in every English session once there are at least 3. Level 1 and 2 ask the child to pick the right spelling from close misspellings made by common slips. Level 3 says the word and asks the child to type it, if typing is allowed for that child. This skill is counted as "Our list" rather than a school year.

## Not covered yet

- Writing, handwriting and composing sentences (LearnLoop only asks children to tap or type short answers).
- Formal phonics assessment. The first-sound and word-reading activities use pictures and are practice, not a phonics check. Windows voices cannot say pure letter sounds reliably, so LearnLoop speaks example words instead.
- Writing at length, and reading papers in the style of the key stage 2 reading test (their texts belong to other authors).
- Area-specific 11+ formats such as the GL Assessment or CEM styles used by particular schools. The Reasoning track is general practice.
- Problems that need drawing or measuring with a ruler or protractor.
